How the labs work

Real repository.
Real evidence.

  • You have write access to one station repository.
  • Green checks are the proof.
  • Each lab feeds the next.

You were given a station repository before the workshop. It already contains the pharmacy stock and reservation service, its tests, its CI workflow, ownership rules and a deliberately wide agentic workflow. You have write access. Everything you do happens there.

The shape of every lab

One directory, one guide
The lab guide and the files it hands you live together. Artifacts are always in artifacts/ next to the guide.
Branch, change, pull request
You never commit to the default branch. It is protected, and Chapter 3 shows you which control protects it.
GitHub decides whether it worked
A green check, a required review, a blocked push or a published issue is the evidence. There is nothing to run locally and no score to print.
Recovery is planned
Every lab has a reference artifact you can copy if you run out of time, so you never lose the rest of the chapter.

Rule

The work accumulates. Lab 2 produces the change that Lab 4 protects and Lab 5 judges. If you fall behind, take the reference artifact and move on rather than skipping a lab.

A browser is enough.

  • Write access to your station repository.
  • Local tooling stays optional throughout.

The core path of all five labs can be completed in the GitHub web interface.

Required
Your station repository URL, write access to it, and a browser.
Optional
A local clone, Git, the gh CLI and a coding harness. Where a command-line equivalent exists, the guide shows it alongside the web path.
Capability dependent
GitHub Agentic Workflows are public preview, and the Copilot coding agent needs an entitlement. Each lab states plainly which step depends on these, and what to do instead when they are unavailable to you.

The sequence

Bound the automation.

  • Narrow tools, permissions and budget.
  • Prove it with a compile check.

Outcome: a merged pull request that narrows the repository-pulse automation to issue review only, at a lower budget, with its compile check green.

You learn why a toolset wider than its permission grant is a silent contradiction, and you discover first-hand what a per-run cap does not give you: continuity.

Open Lab 1

Intent becomes a change.

  • Coached intake, then a structured issue.
  • Acceptance tests judge the result.

Outcome: a real need separated from noisy organizational context with a coaching agent, turned into a structured issue, delegated as a bounded change, and merged once deterministic acceptance tests pass.

This is the change the rest of the day is about. Lab 4 protects it and Lab 5 decides whether it may be released.

Open Lab 2

Make authority visible.

  • Ownership becomes a required review.
  • Watch a protected branch reject you.

Outcome: ownership of the workflow files made explicit and enforced, a direct push observably rejected, and seven governance decisions recorded as a durable artifact.

Stop authority stops being a slide and becomes a rejection message with a name attached to it.

Open Lab 3

Untrusted input is data.

  • Repair a real injection vector.
  • Then attack your own workflow.

Outcome: a hardened delivery workflow that treats a hostile pull request title as data, plus a real code-scanning finding you produced and read.

You prove the repair by attacking it yourself with a safe synthetic payload, and you learn why a passing check never grants release authority.

Open Lab 4

Earn the next action.

  • Bind all evidence to one revision.
  • A justified no-op is an outcome.

Outcome: a written release decision bound to one revision of your own change, naming the evidence relied on, the defects found, and the owner of the next decision.

The evidence you judge is the evidence you produced during the day. The lesson is where its authority ends.

Open Lab 5

Agents propose, deterministic systems produce evidence, and accountable people keep authority over material changes and production release. Each lab makes one part of that sentence observable in your own repository.

Verify the outcome.
Own the next decision.
